Image ads with the hard numbers

Patterns across the ads

  • One look. Every video is a person talking in a real Mesa space, with big word-by-word captions, one highlighted word or number per line, and the same black end card: “PGP in Startup Leadership & Entrepreneurship · Apply Now”.
  • One idea: build, don't study. The ads say “builders” and “real businesses” over and over, set against case studies and MNC jobs. AI is the reason given: those kind of jobs are gone.
  • Three hook types: a number (50–60%, ₹5 lakh, 200 seats), a borrowed authority (investor podcast, investor clip), and a pain point (“stuck in a rut”).
  • Proof is spoken, not shown. Salary jump, startups hiring and student revenue are said or captioned, but no ad cites a placement report, a sample size or a named alumnus with a number.
  • The numbers don't line up. Student business revenue is ₹5 lakh per student (1652092446560613), “80L+ in 8 weeks” (1060943080343883) and “almost one crore” (1443037717945730). Startup employers are “100+” (40210023251944395) and “150+” (1652092446560613). This could be testing or old figures in old ads. Either way, a careful viewer can catch it.
  • Everything is new. The oldest of the 30 is 16 days old. They refresh creative often, or their long-running winners sit outside the 30 the Library showed.

What to run against them

  • Show the proof they only say. Put a verifiable outcome on screen: a named graduate, their before and after, the employer, and the source (placement report, audited figure). Mesa's 50–60% claim has no source. A sourced number answers it without naming Mesa.
  • Take the side Mesa gave up. Mesa says MNC and corporate jobs are “gone”. Most applicants still want a strong job as their safety net. An angle like “build a company, or get hired by the best ones, and we'll prepare you for both” speaks to the people that message leaves out.
  • Use the formats they don't. No student talks first-person about their own result. There is no UGC, no day-in-the-life, nothing about fees or ROI, and nothing in Hindi or Hinglish. All 5 videos are English and from insiders or investors.
  • Answer the borrowed authority. Their strongest ads lean on outside investors. A recruiter, founder or hiring manager saying why they hired your graduate, on camera, with the company named, beats an investor's general endorsement.
  • Meet Ascend head on. “Stuck in a rut” targets working professionals with emotion only. A part-time offer that leads with a concrete outcome (a product shipped in N weeks, a promotion, a raise) would stand out next to it.
  • Keep your own numbers consistent. Pick one figure per claim and use it everywhere, so your ads can't be caught out the way Mesa's can.
