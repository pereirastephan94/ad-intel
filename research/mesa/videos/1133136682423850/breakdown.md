## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work
- The first line states the offer and filters the audience in 4 seconds. "200 people" makes the cohort feel limited without a fake deadline.
- It is dense with numbers a prospect can repeat: 200 people, 12 months, 2,000+ hours, 200+ founders, 10+ problems, 5 lakh.
- Named companies and named tools (Urban Company, Claude, Product Hunt) make "hands-on" concrete for an MBA-entrance audience that mostly hears about case studies.
- Short (37 s of speech), one idea per sentence, a cut on every sentence, and the ad text repeats the same numbers in the same order.
- The spoken CTA, the caption "Apply to Mesa!", the end card and the button all say apply.

Where it is weak
- Every number is asserted, none is shown. No company logos, no student product, no revenue screenshot, no founder faces. There is no b-roll at all.
- Two different "200"s (200 people, 200+ founders) in 15 seconds can blur.
- The speaker has no name or title on screen.
- Nothing about outcomes (jobs, salaries) or cost, which an exam-prep lookalike audience is likely to ask first.
- The link goes to the homepage, not an application page.
- 1 ad, 1 copy. The 9:16 repackaging (1021624400974537) launched 3 days later, so Mesa is still testing shapes for this script.
