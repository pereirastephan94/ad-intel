## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- It opens on the prospect's biggest fear about any MBA (placements) and answers it with "100% placements" inside 6 seconds.
- The speaker is positioned as the person who actually runs careers ("my team"), which is more credible for this topic than a founder or a student.
- The "full-time professionals versus student placecom" contrast is specific and easy to repeat, and it attacks a real practice at Indian B-schools without naming any school.
- "At least 500 startups" and "one-on-one" give the mechanism a number and a process, not just an outcome.
- Documentary look, no music and off-camera eye line make it feel like an interview rather than an ad, which suits a sceptical, older PGP audience.
- Word-by-word captions and the persistent logo make it readable with sound off.

Where it is weak:
- Every headline claim (100% placements, fantastic salaries, 500 startups, team credentials) is asserted with no figure, logo wall, report or named hire on screen. Only one logo (IIMB) appears as proof.
- The speaker is never named or titled on screen, so the viewer has to infer who he is.
- The ad headline ("Your classmates could be your co-founders") and body (peer group, builders) are about a different angle from the video (placements). The message is split.
- Button is "See details" and the link goes to the homepage, not a placements or PGP page, while the end card says "Apply Now". Three different asks.
- The first cut only comes at 3.56 s and the opening is a tight profile of an unnamed man. There is little visual reason to stop scrolling beyond the caption.
- No spoken call to action; the last 6 seconds are a silent black card, which will lose most remaining viewers before the ask.
- The key team-size number is garbled in delivery or caption animation (machine transcript heard "60%"), so the stat may not land clearly.
