## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- The strongest claim comes first, with no preamble: brand name, audience ("alumni") and a salary number inside 3 seconds, with the number in large green type. A viewer who leaves at 3 s has still got the message.
- Salary growth after graduation speaks to the applicant's main doubt about a newer school: what happens to people after they leave. Reporting what managers say, not what the school says about itself, is a sensible way to frame proof.
- Naming four widely known Indian consumer startups gives the "100 plus startups" claim something concrete to hold on to.
- It gives a reason for the outcome (selection, experience, network, building real businesses), which is more credible than outcome alone, and the b-roll of students pitching supports the "build real businesses" part.
- Good sound-off design: stacked captions, green highlights on the number and on key traits, logo fixed top right, and quick b-roll cuts in the first 23 seconds.

Where it is weak:
- The headline figure is unsourced and easy to misread. It is growth one year into the post-Mesa job, with no base, no sample size and no cohort. A careful viewer will ask "50-60% of what?", and nothing on screen answers.
- The manager testimony is second-hand. No manager, employer or alumnus appears or is named. One real manager clip, or even one named quote on screen, would do more than the whole list of traits.
- In the sampled frames no company logos are visible during the list of employers, and that section sits on a single 10.7 s shot. If logos are not shown there, it is a missed chance for proof at the moment the claim is made.
- The second half (33-52 s) turns abstract ("the experience", "people they get to know", "post AI era world") and the pace slows, with a 7.6 s shot near the end. There is no closing restatement of the number and no spoken call to action.
- The speaker is unnamed and untitled, and never looks at the lens.
- The ad copy and headline do not mention salaries, alumni or employers, the button says "See details" while the text and end card say "Apply now", and the link goes to the generic homepage. The last 6 seconds are a static end card that ends in silence.
