## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- It leads with the number MBA applicants care most about (average salary) in the first 3 seconds, and repeats it on a high-contrast card.
- The role titles are specific and aspirational for a startup-minded audience (Founder's Office, Entrepreneur in Residence), which sets Mesa apart from generic "consulting/banking" placement lists.
- The pivot at 16 s ("don't come here just for the jobs") is a takeaway move: it makes the school sound selective and adds a second reason to care, without withdrawing the salary claim.
- The 0.32 s montage at 20.7-23.3 s is a strong retention device midway, at the point where a talking head usually loses viewers, and shows many different students quickly.
- The close ("our super strong careers team will make sure you get a great job") returns to the job outcome, so the ad opens and closes on the benefit.
- The same creative has run since mid-September and has been carried into a new test campaign with 4 copies, which suggests Mesa considers it a performer.

Where it is weak:
- The 25 lakh figure has no source or definition (which batch, median or mean, fixed or CTC). A sceptical applicant or a regulator-minded viewer has nothing to check.
- No company logos or named alumni back the roles or the "Titan Capital, Elevation have hired" line in the frames; it is cards and assertion.
- No running captions are visible in the first 7 seconds, so with sound off the viewer sees only "Avg Salary 25 Lakh+" with no context about whose salary.
- The presenter is not named or titled.
- The ad headline ("Your classmates could be your co-founders") and body text are about the peer group, not outcomes, so the copy and video pull in different directions.
- No spoken call to action, and the end card is 6.5 s of total silence.
- The message has a built-in tension: it sells on salary and then says not to come for jobs. Some viewers may find that confusing rather than persuasive.
