## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- The hook is a concrete event with a recognisable VC name in the first 2.6 s, and a proof image arrives at 1.33 s, before a viewer would normally scroll.
- It shows a real, checkable artefact: an App Store listing with the product name, which is rare in school ads.
- It names the target directly ("if you are 17 years old") and frames the benefit as a head start over "most people" in their 20s, which is easy for a school-leaver or a parent to grasp.
- "Zero tech background" plus Claude Code removes the "I cannot code" objection for a business-degree applicant.
- Fast pacing (4.56 cuts per 10 s, 200 wpm) with two caption styles keeps the eye busy; the logo is on screen the whole time so brand recall does not depend on reaching the end card.
- Square format works across Facebook and Instagram feeds.

Where it is weak:
- The cheque photo shows a Mesa internal prize, not the Elevation pitch. A careful viewer may notice the proof does not match the claim. No outcome of the pitch (funding, feedback) is stated.
- The presenter is never named or titled, so the authority of "our students" rests on production quality alone.
- The Claude Code clip looks like borrowed demo footage with another person's account name on screen, which undercuts authenticity if spotted.
- No ad text, no headline, no fee, deadline or scholarship: nothing creates urgency, and the ad gives no information to someone watching without sound beyond the captions.
- "Before you turn 19" mixes "our students" and "you" and the funds claim ("looking at what students are building") is vague. Three students pitching once is presented as "the baseline" with no number for how many students get this.
- Square, not vertical, so it will not fill the screen in Reels or Stories placements.
