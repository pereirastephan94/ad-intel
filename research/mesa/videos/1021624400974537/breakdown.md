## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- Same proven opening as v1 (number plus "we are choosing"), with a sharper second half: "who do not want to learn business the traditional way" lets the viewer self-select against a normal MBA in the first 4.5 seconds.
- Much more concrete than v1. v1 had one number and no names. v2 gives 12 months, 2,000+ hours, 200+ founders, 10+ problems, 5 lakh, and names four well-known Indian startups and four tools. Each claim is short enough to survive a drop-off.
- The AI and Product Hunt beat speaks to what applicants worry about now, and "real paying users" is a stronger outcome word than "projects".
- The spoken CTA is clean and matched by a caption ("Apply to Mesa!"), which fixes the clipped ending of v1.
- Vertical 9:16 delivery opens Reels and Stories placements that the square v1 fills poorly.
- The ad text repeats the numbers in the same order, so copy and video carry one message.

Where it is weak:
- Still no proof on screen. In the sampled frames no company logo, founder face, student business or product appears while the names are read out. With sound off, the viewer gets captions that stop at "from companies like".
- Only "200" gets a stat card. The other five numbers are easy to miss without sound.
- The vertical version is a square video with blurred bands. About 44% of the screen is blur, so it looks like a repurposed asset next to native vertical content.
- The speaker is not named or given a title.
- "Generate over 5 lakh in revenue" is said as a certainty with no evidence or caveat.
- About 6.5 seconds (15% of the video) is a black frame and static end card.
- It is one day old with one copy on one audience, so there is no sign yet of how Mesa rates it. The landing page is the generic homepage.
