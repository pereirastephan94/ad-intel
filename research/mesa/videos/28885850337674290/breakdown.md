## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work
- A tiny, odd-looking number in the first second, typed on screen as he says it, is a strong stop for anyone who has "learn AI" on their list.
- The four "because" reasons describe the viewer's own excuses (too technical, too many tools, tutorials, no guide), so Mesa's answer lands as a fix for a felt problem.
- Proof is told as three short student stories, the first with specifics (investment banker, 50+ VCs, 1000+ users). The headline "Non-tech doesn't mean non-builder" matches that first story.
- The close reframes the stakes (built versus not built) and puts Mesa on the right side of it. Ad text repeats the video's own lines, so feed and video agree.
- This is the only version of the script framed for the full phone screen, and it carries 7 copies against 1 each for the banded and square versions. Mesa is putting more delivery behind this cut, though no results are in this data.

Where it is weak
- "0.001%" has no source and is easy to challenge. A sceptical viewer may stop trusting the rest.
- None of the three students is named or shown with their product. The b-roll does not match the stories (a woman with a mic appears under "pitched it to 50+ VCs").
- No AI product, screen recording or demo appears anywhere in a video about building AI products.
- The speaker has no name or title on screen.
- No spoken call to action, and the ad text ends on "Get the details" while the button and end card say apply.
- The link goes to the homepage, where the viewer has to find the AI curriculum on their own.
