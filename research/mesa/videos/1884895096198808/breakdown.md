## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- The before-and-after is complete within 7 s (UPSC prep to working with a founder), so the viewer knows the payoff before any detail.
- UPSC and KPMG are both widely understood reference points for the target audience; the story speaks to people who feel they lost years to exam prep or are stuck in a services job.
- The proof chain is specific and causal: a named live project (Two Brothers Organic Farms), a concrete thing she built (an AI agent with three stated functions), and a named outcome (EiR at GoDesi). It shows how the job came about, not only that it did.
- "I was genuinely scared of technology" handles the objection of non-technical viewers before the AI claim.
- A second hook at 27.6 s ("here's the interesting part") and denser cutting from 23 to 45 s help hold attention in a 76 s video.
- A peer speaking to the lens, with captions on every line, works with sound off; the ad text tells the same story in the third person.

Where it is weak:
- The 9:16 version is a square video with blurred bars. Close to half of the screen is empty, the subject is small, and the captions sit in the middle of the phone screen. It reads as a resize, not a native vertical.
- Her name, employer and title never appear on screen; the viewer has to read the ad text to learn she is Apoorva at GoDesi. No lower-third, no company logos.
- No cut in the first 10 s and no Mesa branding until 10.3 s; the school is not identified visually at the start.
- The key proof is told, not shown: no view of the AI agent, the outreach, an offer letter or her at work. The founder photo is unlabelled in the frames.
- No numbers on outcome (salary, role level, time to offer) and no cohort-level data, so it is one story.
- 76 s is long for a cold lookalike audience, and the last 5 s is a silent static card.
- Little or no music, by the software estimate, which can make a long talking piece feel flat.
