## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work
- It speaks to the parent, the person who pays for an undergraduate degree, and says so in the first three words. Every benefit is phrased "your child will".
- The opening frame is a face at full size with an open mouth and a pointing hand, then three cuts in 5 s. It is built to stop a scroll.
- Short (35 s) and dense: one benefit, one matching shot, about every second. It has the highest cut rate in the batch (5.94 per 10 s).
- The black and red title cards give three takeaways readable with sound off: build real businesses, sell to real customers, 1-on-1 mentorship.
- B-roll shows the claim being done (a student taking a payment), which is stronger than a presenter saying it.
- Clear spoken ask plus an end card that answers two parent questions at once: it is on campus, and it is in Bangalore.

Where it is weak
- The questions parents ask first are not answered: is it a recognised degree, how long, what it costs, what happens after. None of this is in the video and the ad has no text or headline to cover it.
- "Founder or a CEO" is a large promise with no outcome proof. No alumni, no numbers, no named mentors.
- The presenter is not named and gives no credentials, so a parent does not know who is making the promise.
- "1-on-1 mentorship" is illustrated with a group talk, which does not match the claim.
- Empty ad text and headline and a link with no tracking suggest a test or a quick launch (started 2026-11-18, 1 copy). There is no sign of scale in this data.
- The pace suits students more than parents. A parent weighing a multi-year decision may want one slower, concrete proof point.
