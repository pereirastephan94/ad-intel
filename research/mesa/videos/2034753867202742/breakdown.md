## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- It names one narrow audience (software developers with about 10 years of experience) in the first 2 s and in the headline, so the right viewer self-selects and others scroll on.
- The fear is specific and current: AI automating code, and not being able to "justify his salary" in a new function. That is a concrete worry, not a general career message.
- Third-person story ("a developer I know") lets the viewer recognise himself without being told he is in trouble.
- The numbered list of four gives a reason to watch to the end and the list maps one-to-one onto what the programme sells (business basics, revenue project, network, building with AI). It reads as useful advice, so it does not feel like an ad until the end card.
- It flatters the audience near the end ("naturally analytical and great at problem solving"), which makes the change feel achievable.
- Fast pace (178 words per minute, a cut every 2 to 3 s) with a music or sound bed, and captions on screen for sound-off viewing.

Where it is weak:
- The video never says Mesa or the programme name aloud and never connects the four steps to the programme. The whole link is left to the ad text and a 6 s end card, so viewers who do not read the text may take the advice and leave.
- No proof of any kind: no alumni who made this switch, no outcomes, no numbers, and the developer in the story is unverifiable.
- The speaker is unnamed and untitled, so there is no stated reason to trust his career advice.
- The end card omits "Ascend", "Hybrid", "weekends" and "12 months", which are the facts a working developer needs (can I do this without quitting?).
- 74 s is long; the fourth point arrives at 48.7 s and the payoff at 63 s.
- Captions carry no highlight colours and several frames at cut points show no caption, so the sound-off experience is uneven at the cuts.
- "AI is automating the code completely" is an overstatement that a technical audience may reject, which risks credibility with exactly the people targeted.
