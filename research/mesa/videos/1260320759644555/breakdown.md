## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- "CAT 2025" in the first 2 seconds, in bold on screen, selects exactly the audience Mesa wants: MBA aspirants who sat the exam, many of whom will be unhappy with their score when the ad runs.
- Showing a real-looking exam question is a strong pattern break and makes the argument tangible. Most viewers will agree at a glance that it has little to do with business.
- "We don't care how much you scored in just one CAT exam" removes the single biggest barrier for low scorers and reframes them as skilled rather than rejected.
- It is short (33.7 s, speech done by 29 s), has one idea, a spoken CTA and a matching end card. Easy to follow with sound off thanks to captions and the question card.
- Heavy branding (large corner logo plus the polo logo) means even a 3-second view carries the Mesa name.
- Running the same video under two creative names (cat-aa and standardised-tests) suggests Mesa is testing labels or placements for this angle.

Where it is weak:
- It never says what Mesa's own selection test is, so "that is exactly what Mesa tests for" is an unsupported promise.
- No outcome proof at all: no placements, alumni, founders or numbers. It argues against CAT but gives no reason to believe Mesa is a good alternative.
- The ad headline (AI agents and no-code) and body (peer cohort) do not match the video's message (CAT does not matter). Three different angles in one ad.
- The speaker is not named or titled, so a viewer cannot tell whether this is a founder, a dean or an actor.
- Only 6 cuts and no b-roll; after the question card at 3 s the visual is one man talking, and the final 9.4 s runs on a single shot. Retention relies on his delivery and the caption animation.
- Dismissing CAT with "Zero. None." may put off high scorers and parents who see the exam as a mark of rigour, and can make Mesa look like a fallback for low scorers.
- A fully black frame at 29 s before the end card can read as the video ending, prompting a scroll before the CTA card appears.
