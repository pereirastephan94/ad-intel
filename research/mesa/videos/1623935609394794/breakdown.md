## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- It attacks the standard objection to a new school (faculty quality) by borrowing the standing of a top US school. For an audience with 2 to 8 years of experience, "a Kellogg professor teaches here" is a credential they already understand.
- The opening line sounds like gossip, not a pitch, and the speaker is looking at an interviewer, not the lens. That lowers the "this is an ad" reaction.
- It is told as one story about one person with a twist (he could teach anywhere in India, he chose here, and here is why). Stories hold attention better than feature lists, which helps a 78 s runtime.
- The IPL ad review is a concrete, culturally local session that a viewer can picture and would want to attend. The cricket ad clips are familiar and visually loud.
- The edit works hard to hold viewers: 52 cuts, b-roll matched to almost every phrase, a globe animation, and two sub-second montages late in the video where drop-off is usually worst.
- Captions with green highlight words carry the story with sound off.

Where it is weak:
- The key evidence is hearsay. The professor never speaks, is never named on screen in the sampled frames, and no Kellogg logo appears. A viewer has to take the speaker's word for the quote.
- "Professors" is plural in the hook, but only one is given. "Best professor in the world" and "best marketing school in the world" are unsupported superlatives.
- The speaker himself is not named or titled, yet the story depends on his personal access ("I asked him one day").
- No call to action in the script, no outcome data, no numbers. It builds belief in teaching quality but does not tell the viewer what to do or what they get.
- The ad text and headline are about a different message (200 seats, built for builders). Someone who reads before watching gets no cue that the video is about faculty.
- At 78 s and 203 words per minute it asks a lot. The payoff (the ad review) starts at 47 s.
- Brand presence is light until the end: only a small "m" tile, with no wordmark. A viewer who leaves before 26 s may not register which school this is unless they hear "Mesa".
- Some b-roll is dim and grainy, and the TV ad clips are third-party footage, which may be a rights exposure if copied.
