## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- The hook has three specifics in 8 s: age (17), a familiar product (khakra), and a monthly revenue figure. It is easy to repeat to a parent or friend.
- The proof is physical and local: real packs with a brand label, a stall, a cheque, an Instagram page the viewer could look up.
- The list of tasks (supplier, inventory, cash, website, B2B, ads) doubles as a syllabus. It tells the applicant what they would actually do, in plain verbs.
- "Irrespective of whether the business becomes super successful" pre-empts the parent's objection that a student snack brand is not a career, and moves the value to skills and confidence.
- "Every single student does [this] in the first semester" turns one success story into a claim about the course.
- Vertical full-frame, first cut at 2.1 s, frequent picture changes early, large readable captions: it is built for Reels viewing.

Where it is weak:
- The headline number, 20 lakh a month, has no evidence on screen, and a sister ad says 25 lakh for what appears to be the same brand. The inconsistency undermines both.
- The Instagram Reel shown has 54 likes, which sits oddly beside the revenue claim for anyone who reads the screenshot.
- The students do not speak and are not named; the brand name is never said. The viewer cannot easily search for it unless they read the label.
- The speaker has no name or title on screen.
- The ads have no text and no headline, so there is nothing for a sound-off scroller beyond the captions, and nothing that states the offer in writing.
- No spoken call to action and no deadline; the last 6 s are a silent end card.
- The close goes abstract ("India ... superpower") and attacks BBA colleges with an unsupported "100 years old" line, which gives the 17-year-old viewer no next step.
- White caption text on cream cards (13.8-19 s) is hard to read.
