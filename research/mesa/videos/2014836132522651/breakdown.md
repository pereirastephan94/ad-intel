## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- It leads with the number most applicants ask about first (salary), stated in the first sentence and shown as a large label by 3 s.
- The roles list is specific to startups (Founder's Office, Entrepreneur in Residence), which signals a different outcome from a standard MBA placement list.
- Naming two venture firms as employers adds borrowed credibility in 4 s.
- "Don't come here just for the jobs" works as a reverse pitch: it screens for motivated applicants and makes the job claim sound less like a sales line, then the last sentence gives the job reassurance anyway.
- The 0.32 s montage of many different students presenting gives a sense of scale and activity, and is the fastest-cut section of any of these ads.
- Short: the talk is done in 38 s at 158 words per minute.

Where it is weak:
- This version wastes close to half the phone screen on blurred bands. The speaker is small, hand gestures are cut off, and the logo and captions float in the middle of the screen. A full-height version of the same edit exists.
- In the visible frame the roles stack shows two labels where the full-height cut shows four, so the roles proof is weaker on screen here.
- The salary figure has no source or definition, and no employer names or logos back the roles or the VC claim.
- The speaker is not named or titled, so a cold viewer does not know who is making the salary claim.
- No captions in the first 2 s, so a sound-off viewer sees an unnamed man talking with no text until the salary label appears.
- No spoken call to action, and 6.5 s of silent end card (about 15% of the runtime).
- The headline ("Your classmates could be your co-founders") and ad text (peer group) do not match the video's subject (salary and jobs).
