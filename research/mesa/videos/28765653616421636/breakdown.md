## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- It targets the main worry of an MBA applicant (will I get a job) and names it in the first 5 seconds, then answers with the strongest possible claim, "100% placements".
- It explains a mechanism, not just an outcome: a paid full-time careers team, contact with 500 startups, curriculum revised from employer needs, one-on-one interview prep. That gives the viewer a reason to believe the outcome.
- The contrast with "other business schools" where students run placements themselves is specific and will be recognised by anyone who knows how placement committees work. It positions without naming a competitor.
- The interview framing (off-camera eye line, laughing at 37 s, casual shirt) reads as candid, not scripted. The b-roll of full classrooms and staff adds evidence that the place and people exist.
- Captions carry the argument with sound off, and the green highlight and the IIMB logo pull the eye to the credential lines. The pace picks up to a cut every 2 seconds once the proof section starts.

Where it is weak:
- The first 1.6 seconds are "You know, people ask me", which says nothing. Mesa also runs a cut of this same video with that line removed (asset 1136750059311367), which suggests they are testing exactly this.
- The headline numbers have no backing on screen in the sampled frames: no cohort year for "100% placements", no salary figure, no employer logos, no named team member. A sceptical viewer has only the speaker's word, and he is unnamed and untitled.
- "People ask me" sets up a question, but the next line is a statement, so the opening sentence does not quite parse.
- 61.7 seconds is long, and the pay-off ("no comparison") is a repeat of the claim, not a new reason. There is no spoken call to action, and the last 6 seconds are a silent end card that uses only the top half of the vertical frame.
- The ad copy and headline ("Your classmates could be your co-founders") do not match the video's message, and the link goes to the generic homepage, not a placements page. A lead-gen campaign on an exam-database lookalike would likely do better with copy that repeats the placement claim.
- The exact team size is hard to read in the caption animation (blurred green text), which weakens one of the few concrete numbers.
