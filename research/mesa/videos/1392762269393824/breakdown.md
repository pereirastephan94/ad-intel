## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- "UPSC" in the first sentence speaks to a huge group of Indian graduates who have spent years on civil-service prep and fear those years are wasted. The before/after is stated within 7 seconds.
- A named student with a specific path (UPSC, KPMG, Mesa, EiR at GoDesi) and named brands is more believable than a school spokesperson making general claims.
- The story has a concrete cause and effect: she built a specific AI agent, and that agent got her the interview. This shows the programme's AI/no-code teaching producing a job, not just a certificate.
- "Genuinely scared of technology" handles the objection of non-technical applicants in the student's own words.
- The ad text and one headline ("From KPMG to EiR at GoDesi") tell the same story as the video, so copy and creative reinforce each other. A second headline tests a cohort/urgency angle.
- Clear spoken CTA that names the programme and its length, followed by a matching end card.
- Direct eye contact, captions and a 167 wpm pace make it easy to follow with or without sound.

Where it is weak:
- No Mesa logo is visible anywhere before the end card at about 71 s. A sound-off viewer who leaves early will not know which school this is unless they read the ad text.
- Her name and current title are never shown on screen. A lower-third ("Apoorva, EiR at GoDesi, ex-KPMG") would do a lot of work and is missing in the frames.
- The strongest claims (the AI agent, the GoDesi role) have no visual proof. The only cutaway evidence is a public website of a different brand.
- No cut for the first 9 seconds and only one camera; the tight punch-ins are visibly soft. The first second is also quieter than the rest.
- 76 seconds is long; the outcome with the company name arrives at 41 to 50 s. The UPSC angle is strong but is dropped until the 52 s recap.
- No salary, no numbers on outcomes, no deadline. Nothing creates urgency.
- Square format will not fill the screen in Reels or Stories placements.
