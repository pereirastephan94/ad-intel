## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- It speaks to a large, specific and anxious audience: CAT takers. "We don't care how much you scored in just one CAT exam" removes the main barrier for people with a weak score.
- Showing a real-looking exam question and then asking what it tests is a simple, concrete argument that needs no numbers.
- The Harvard MBA card in the first second gives a reason to listen to a critique of exams from someone with a top credential.
- The intro has two cuts and movement in the first 2 s, which the plain talking-head opening of the earlier cut lacks.
- Direct eye contact, an emphatic delivery and a short spoken CTA; the whole talk is under 30 s.
- A single-minded message: skills over one test score.

Where it is weak:
- The CAT hook, which is the reason to keep watching, is pushed back to 3 s. A viewer who does not care about a name card may leave before it.
- The speaker's role at Mesa is never stated; the card gives only his MBA.
- No proof of outcomes: no placements, salaries, alumni or admission process details.
- The ad text and headline (cohort of builders, AI agents and no-code) do not match the video's CAT message, so the feed unit is pulling in two directions.
- The link goes to the homepage, not a programme or application page, which adds a step after a "apply now" ask.
- About 5 s of black and end card at the end (14% of the runtime) with no new information such as deadlines or eligibility.
- The plain captions are thin and small, and sit over the chest and logo, so they are harder to read with sound off than the bold name card.
