## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work
- The first second carries a hard credential (200+ startups, 800 crores) in voice, caption and graphic at once, so it reads even with sound off.
- The speech is framed as advice to founders, not a pitch to students. A prospective student overhears an investor saying "hire from them", which is third-party proof of job outcomes instead of the school praising itself.
- The mechanism is specific and easy to repeat: 8 weeks, 5 lakhs, sell a real product. It gives a concrete picture of what the course is.
- The problem section lists the skills employers want, which doubles as a checklist of what the student will become.
- Podcast-set look and off-camera eye line make it feel like a clip from an interview, not an ad. The campus shot proves he actually went there.
- Fast speech (209 wpm) with a cut every 2 to 3 s keeps density high for a long 82 s video.

Where it is weak
- No call to action is spoken, and the end card is silent. The student is never addressed directly in the video.
- The speaker says the school was "started by my friends", which weakens his independence as an endorser. Any commercial relationship is not disclosed in the frames or ad text.
- "150+ startups are already hiring" and the founders' Harvard and Kellogg backgrounds are stated with no logos, names or data on screen.
- The headline ("AI agents & no-code") and the video (hiring, selling 5 lakhs) are about different things, so the message between video and headline is split.
- The speaker is never identified on screen. Viewers who do not recognise him have only his own numbers to go on.
- No fees, dates, eligibility, placement figures or salary numbers anywhere. The link goes to the homepage, not a dedicated application page.
