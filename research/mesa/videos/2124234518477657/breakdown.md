## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- The first 8 s are someone else's bold claim, not a school advert. That delays the "this is an ad" reaction and gives the Mesa speaker something to agree with instead of something to prove.
- "Finally, somebody said it" turns the pitch into a reaction, which is a familiar native format on Reels.
- One clear argument runs the whole length: companies shrink, MNC jobs go, only two roles remain, both need building skills, Mesa teaches them. Each step sets up the next.
- The selfie with a clip mic looks low-cost and personal, while the b-roll of real students selling and presenting gives some visual evidence for "build a real business".
- The body text of variant B repeats the video's argument in writing, so the message survives with sound off or if the viewer only reads.
- It is being reused: 4 ads, 7 copies, in both awareness and lead-gen campaigns and against two landing pages.

Where it is weak:
- The biggest numbers ("95% of jobs", "30 employees", "almost one crore") have no source or proof on screen. A sceptical viewer can dismiss them.
- No Mesa person is named or titled on screen, so the viewer does not know why to trust the speaker.
- 105 s is long for a cold audience and Mesa is not mentioned until 57 s. The only brand cue before that is a small logo that is often unreadable on light backgrounds.
- No stat cards or headline text; a sound-off viewer gets only 1 to 3 word captions and cannot skim the argument.
- In this square version the live picture is small inside a grey card, so faces and captions are small on a phone.
- The call to action has no reason to act now beyond "as quickly as possible": no deadline, no date, no next step such as a call or an event.
