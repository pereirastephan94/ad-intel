## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- Precise audience and a real fear. "Software developer", "10 years", "FAANG", "feels stuck", "AI is automating the code" will be recognised at once by experienced engineers, and the ad runs against a tech and AI interest audience, so message and targeting match.
- The story form lowers resistance. It is about "a developer I know", not "you", and it flatters the audience (top companies, hardest problems, "naturally analytical") before giving advice.
- A numbered list of four gives a reason to keep watching, and the four points are the programme's selling points in disguise. The ad text then states the link openly ("At Mesa, you learn the 4 things that matter now").
- The headline "Engineers: Learn the Business Side" and the ad text match the video closely, which is better alignment than Mesa's other homepage PGP ads in this batch.
- Advice point 2 is concrete (D2C product, suppliers, inventory, website, offline sales) and is backed by stall footage, so the "real revenue" idea is easy to picture.
- One interview shoot has produced at least three ads for two programmes (this one for PGP, two for Ascend), which is an efficient production model.

Where it is weak:
- Mesa is never spoken and there is no spoken call to action. The link between the advice and the school exists only on the end card and in the ad text; a viewer who stops at 67 s has watched free advice.
- Possible audience and product mismatch. The story is about a developer with 10 years of experience and a high salary, but the product on the end card is the full-time PGP, which would mean leaving that job. Mesa's part-time programme is not mentioned. The video does not address this.
- No proof and no resolution. The developer is anonymous, his outcome is not told, and no engineer who took the programme is shown.
- The speaker has no name or title on screen, so his standing to advise senior engineers is not established.
- "AI is automating the code completely" is an absolute statement that a technical audience may reject, which risks losing the very viewers the hook attracts.
- Several frames carry no caption (7.9 s, 14.1 s, 21.8 s) and only "#3" of the four numbered markers is confirmed in the frames, so the list structure may be less clear with sound off. The software found little or no music, and about 6.5 s (9%) is a fully silent end card.
