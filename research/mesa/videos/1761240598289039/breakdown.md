## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work
- It opens with a recognised voice saying something provocative, so the first 8 seconds do not look or sound like a school ad.
- "Finally, somebody said it" is a reaction format native to Reels. The selfie, clip-on mic and corridor make it feel like a personal take.
- The argument is a clear chain: companies shrink, MNC jobs go, two roles remain, you need builder skills, schools do not teach them, Mesa does. Each step sets up the next.
- Direct eye contact with the lens for about 90 seconds, unlike the interview-style videos in this account.
- It has a spoken call to action with mild urgency ("still accepting applications", "as quickly as possible"), and the ad text, headline and video all make the same argument.
- The fear angle (AI taking 95% of MNC jobs) targets the exact people weighing a traditional MBA.

Where it is weak
- The key numbers (30 employees, 95% of MNC jobs, almost one crore revenue) have no source or on-screen proof. "95 percent" in particular is an extreme claim that invites disbelief.
- It uses a third party's clip and name. Nothing in the frames shows permission or that he endorses Mesa, which is a rights and implied-endorsement risk for anyone copying the format.
- At 105 s with a slow, zero-cut first 5 seconds and small thin captions, it depends on sound and on the viewer knowing who the clip speaker is.
- Mesa is not mentioned by voice until 56 s. Viewers who drop before then get the thesis but not the offer.
- The speaker is not identified, and there is no student or employer voice.
- Launched 2026-11-18 with 1 copy, so there is no sign yet in this data that it is being scaled. The creative itself is dated 11-09-2026 in its name, so it is being reused in a new campaign.
