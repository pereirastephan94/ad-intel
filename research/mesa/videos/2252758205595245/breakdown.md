## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- It opens on a result a 17-year-old or parent can check in 30 seconds: a named app on the App Store. Checkable proof is rare in education ads.
- The programme is named in the first 2 s, highlighted in red, with the logo on screen and on the shirt, so the right audience is selected at once.
- "Non-technical background" removes the main objection for a business-degree applicant ("I can't code") and turns the app from a one-off into evidence about the course.
- The mechanism is specific and name-drops well-known AI companies and a current tool (OpenAI, Anthropic, Emergent, Claude Code), which signals an up-to-date curriculum.
- At 62 s with a 2.4 s first cut and cutaways on each claim, it is short and visually varied for a talking head.
- 18 copies launched on the same day, 13 on one ad, suggests it is being pushed hard at launch (no performance data is available to confirm results).

Where it is weak:
- "Thousands of users" sits next to an App Store screen showing "4 RATINGS". An attentive viewer may see the mismatch.
- The two students never speak and are never named. The story is told about them by a school representative, which is weaker than hearing it from them.
- The speaker has no name or title on screen.
- The ending is an attack ("no BBA college ... absolutely shocking", "graduates that are absolutely useless") with no evidence. It may put off parents and it ends the spoken part on a negative, not on what to do next.
- No spoken call to action, no deadline or admissions detail; the last 6 s are a silent card.
- Hackathon and mentor claims are illustrated only with generic classroom footage; no partner logos or event shots.
- Links have no utm tags, so the advertiser cannot attribute site visits to this creative by link (an operational weakness, not a creative one).
