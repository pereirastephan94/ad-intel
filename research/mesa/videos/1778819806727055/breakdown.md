## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work
- The first sentence names the audience and its fear with a number, and the gold "90%" lands in the first half second.
- It is built as advice, not a pitch. Four numbered steps are useful on their own, which earns watch time and saves, and each step maps to a part of the programme without saying so.
- "27 to 32 year olds" is an exact age call-out that matches the 25 to 38 targeting, so the right viewer feels singled out.
- "And you know why?" at 10 s is an open loop that carries viewers past the first drop-off point.
- The ad text mirrors the video's four steps one for one and then attaches Ascend, so the text does the selling the video leaves out.
- 6 copies of a single ad suggests it is being run across several ad sets.

Where it is weak
- "90%" and "100% going to become irrelevant" have no source. They are easy to dismiss and could draw complaints.
- The video never says Mesa, Ascend, weekends, 12 months or who it is for. Without the ad text, a viewer gets advice and a logo.
- The close is a threat ("become irrelevant") followed by a silent end card. There is no spoken ask and no benefit stated.
- No proof that the programme delivers the four steps: no student, project, outcome or employer shown or named.
- The speaker is unnamed and gives no credentials for giving career advice.
- First 5 seconds have no cut and he looks off-camera, so the opening leans entirely on the caption and the number.
