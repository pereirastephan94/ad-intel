## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- The hook is a line many students and parents already believe, addressed to "you", and it names the target age ("17, 18 years old") in the first 7 s, so the right viewer self-selects.
- It sells the peer group, not the syllabus. Four concrete student stories in 19 s (TV pitch, 2 crore grant, 25 lakh a month, apps earning dollars) make "extraordinary students" tangible and give different viewers different stories to relate to.
- "Less than 5% ... get an offer" plus a multi-step process signals selectivity, which raises perceived status and frames applying as a challenge.
- The admissions steps are spelled out on screen as a clear list, which also tells the applicant what happens after they click.
- It has a spoken call to action, a matching headline ("Find Your Tribe") and body text that restate the same single idea.
- Fast b-roll (a cut about every second for 38 s) and a polished two-camera look keep a talking-head ad moving. Launch weight is high: 20 copies on day one, 13 on one ad.

Where it is weak:
- It is landscape 16:9. In Facebook and Instagram feeds, Reels and Stories it will take a small part of a phone screen, and the thin bottom captions and small logo tag will be hard to read.
- None of the student claims is proved on screen: no names, no show or incubator logos, no app listings, no revenue evidence. Proper nouns are only spoken, so a sound-off viewer gets none of them.
- The khakra revenue figure (25 lakh) conflicts with the 20 lakh quoted in a sister ad launched the same day.
- "Five step" process, four steps shown and named.
- The speaker is not named or titled.
- With cuts every second and one shot per story, each example passes too fast to register who the student is; the students never speak.
- The 5% figure has no base (applicants, seats or year), and there is no deadline to give "apply now" urgency.
