## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work:
- The Kellogg comparison is the strongest device: a speaker with a top global MBA says he saw two CEOs from afar in two years, against 100+ founders in a room in 12 months. It is concrete, personal and flattering to Mesa without attacking an Indian competitor.
- "We just don't do fireside chats. Those are boring." is a quotable contrast that names what other schools do and positions Mesa's version as different.
- The mechanism is a clear chain: founder problem, written case, student solution, presentation, job offer. It links founder access directly to the outcome MBA applicants care about.
- "Almost 40% of our students get jobs through this medium" gives a specific, modest-sounding number, which reads as more believable than a 100% claim.
- Ad text adds scarcity ("selecting 200") and matches the video's theme (trained by founders), so the message is consistent.
- Targeting (XAT lookalike, per campaign name) fits a message that compares against conventional MBAs.

Where it is weak:
- No cut for 7.88 s and no b-roll until after 31 s. The key number ("100 plus founders") lands at about 5 s over a static shot of an unnamed man, with no founder faces or logos to prove it.
- No founder, company or hired student is ever shown or named. The video is all tell and no show for a claim that would be easy to evidence.
- The speaker is not named or titled, and Kellogg is only spoken, so the authority that the comparison depends on is not anchored on screen.
- 68 seconds at 2.49 cuts per 10 s is slow; the 40% stat arrives at 53 s, after most viewers will have left.
- No spoken call to action and a silent 5 s black end card. The link goes to the homepage rather than the PGP page.
- Square format will not fill the screen on Reels, Stories, WhatsApp Status or Threads placements listed in the ad.
