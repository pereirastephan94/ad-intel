## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Why it may work
- The hook names an exact age and an exact job type, so the target viewer (mid-20s, in a support or operations role at a big company) knows at once it is about them.
- Loss framing: "you are falling behind" and "AI will take over most of these roles" give a reason to act now, before the school is mentioned.
- The structure is a simple before and after ("it's the exact opposite"), and each "you will" line gets its own b-roll shot, so the list never sits on one picture.
- Captions do real work: green for the age, red for "worst", a green box for "200+". The first frame is an extreme close-up, which stops the scroll.
- Proof footage shows real student stalls with real product names and prices, which backs "building real businesses" better than a claim alone.
- Mesa is named at 31.6 s, after the viewer has agreed with the problem.

Where it is weak
- "The worst thing you can do" may put off the same people it targets, and their employers. It attacks a choice the viewer has already made.
- No number is sourced: 200+, 5 lakhs and "top roles in great startups" have no names, logos or figures on screen.
- The speaker has no name or title on screen, so "Just ask me" has no weight for a cold viewer. (It is a retargeting ad, so some viewers may already know him.)
- Headline ("The only B-school teaching AI agents & no-code") and ad text (cohort and co-founders) are about different things from the video (startup versus MNC). Three messages in one ad.
- 214 words per minute is very fast, and the key word in the hook was misheard even by transcription software.
- 1 ad, 1 copy, started 2026-11-19, so there is no sign yet of scaling.
