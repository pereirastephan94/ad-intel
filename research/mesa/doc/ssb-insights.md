These points compare Mesa's ads with SSB's own script rules (the 7-beat structure, one number every 25 to 31 words, named companies, one call to action with three urgency levers). SSB's current scripts and results were not available for this draft, so treat these as a brief to test, not a verdict.

### Where SSB can beat Mesa

| Mesa's gap | What SSB already has | What to do in the script |
|---|---|---|
| No number is sourced, and figures conflict between ads | Median CTC, salary hike multiple, 1,200+ hiring partners, 10 years in education | Put the source on screen beside each number. Say the cohort the number belongs to |
| Speakers are anonymous in 33 of 35 videos | Faculty who are operators from named companies | Name and title on screen in the first 5 seconds |
| Proof is told, not shown | Company projects with named brands (Practo, Mokobara) and founder feedback | Show the student deck, the founder in the room, the result |
| 22 of 35 videos have no spoken call to action; none gives a deadline | SSB's rule: one call to action stacking seats, deadline and scholarship | Speak the ask. Show it on the end card with the date |
| Only one student ever speaks | Students and alumni | Lead at least one video in three with a student in their own words |
| Ad headline and video often do not match | n/a | Write the headline and first line together, per video |
| Half the videos are not true vertical; Ascend has none | n/a | Shoot vertical first, cut square from it |

### What to borrow from Mesa

- **The plain talking head.** Mesa's most-used video is 38 seconds, one person, one office, no extra footage. Cheap to make and easy to vary. SSB should have a set of these.
- **Text on screen by half a second, a number by three seconds.** Mesa does this in most videos. It fits SSB's number-density rule.
- **One shoot, many ads.** One interview session (the maroon polo shoot) gave Mesa 7 ads across two programmes. Plan each SSB shoot as 8 to 10 short answers, each a standalone ad.
- **One audience per video.** Mesa has separate videos for developers, family-business owners, mid-career managers and MBA-exam takers. The call-out is in the first line.
- **A colour per programme**, so a viewer knows which programme an ad is for before reading.
- **Casual clothing.** Polo or plain shirt, no suit. It reads as an operator, not a brochure.

### What not to copy

- **Zoom-only openings and "You know," as a first word.** 14 of 35 Mesa videos have no cut in the first 5 seconds and 5 start with "You know,". The first second is wasted.
- **Advice videos that never name the school.** 4 of 6 Ascend videos never say the product aloud.
- **Attacking the MBA head-on.** Mesa says "Not another MBA". SSB's own post-mortem found that this frame makes MBA aspirants defensive.
- **Unsourced big numbers.** They are Mesa's weakest point and the easiest thing for a viewer to doubt.

### The Ascend threat

- Mesa's largest push is for working professionals who will not quit. If SSB wants that audience, "keep your job" is now Mesa's line and it is saying it loudly.
- Ascend has no graduates yet. Its proof is borrowed from the full-time programme. SSB can answer with real outcomes from people who studied while working, if it has them.
- Mesa's own ads disagree on whether Ascend is "Online" or "Hybrid", and on whether early bird closes on 26 or 29 November. A clear, consistent offer is an advantage by itself.

### A suggested first test for SSB

1. Six plain talking-head videos, 35 to 45 seconds, vertical, one audience each, named speaker, number on screen by second 2, spoken call to action with a date.
2. Two student-led videos showing a named company project.
3. One longer video (about 250 words) on the 7-beat structure for warm audiences.
4. Image ads that put the deadline and the scholarship on the image itself, not only in the text.
