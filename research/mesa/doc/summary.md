**Mesa is in the middle of a launch push, not a steady campaign.** Most of its live ads are less than a week old, about half of them sell a brand-new part-time programme called Ascend, and nearly every video is the same thing: one person talking in a Mesa office.

### The ten things to know

1. **A sudden ramp.** 114 of the 163 ads collected started on 16, 17 or 18 November.
2. **Ascend is the push.** About half the ads and 53 of the 71 image ads sell Ascend, a weekend programme for people aged 25 to 38. Its whole message is "keep your job".
3. **One video format.** All 35 videos are a person talking, in an office or on a podcast set. The 35 files are only about 22 different edits; the rest are re-cuts.
4. **The plainest video is the most used.** "We are looking for 200 special people" is 38 seconds, one man in a maroon polo, no extra footage, no proof. It runs in 7 ads with 13 copies.
5. **Numbers open the videos.** 12 of 35 open on a statistic and 8 on a story. Only 2 open on a question. Text is on screen within half a second in 30 of 35.
6. **Casual on purpose.** Polo shirts in 23 of 35, casual shirts in 10. No suits. 20 of 35 speakers never look at the camera; they are filmed as if answering an interviewer.
7. **Fast talk, moderate cutting.** The typical video is 61 seconds, 171 words a minute, about 3 cuts every 10 seconds. The undergraduate videos cut fastest.
8. **Each programme has a colour.** Gold for Ascend, green for the full-time PGP, crimson for the undergraduate programme.
9. **They rarely ask.** 22 of 35 videos have no spoken call to action. 7 never say "Mesa" aloud. No video states a fee, a deadline or a next step. Each ends on a silent black card.
10. **Proof is thin.** No number is sourced on screen. Speakers are named in only 2 of 35 videos. Figures conflict between ads (startup partners are 100, 150 and 500; one student brand earns 20 lakh a month in one ad and 25 in another).

### What is not known

- Spend and results are not public, so this shows what Mesa is betting on, not what is working.
- The pull is still running. The counts at the top of the page are current; the findings below were written on the first 163 ads.
- Music is measured by software. About half the videos seem to have a music bed; tracks cannot be named.
