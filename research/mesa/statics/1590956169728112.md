## Hook and message
A Mesa graduate got a Founder's Office job abroad. It is for MBA-exam aspirants and career switchers who want a startup role and like the idea of working overseas. The promise: the PGP gives you the skills to move from an unrelated field (here, architecture) into a Founder's Office role in Dubai.

## Claims and proof
- 'An International Founder's Office role.' Supported by a named student (Om Kadam, Cohort 3) and a named employer chip (Rechitta). No salary, no date, no quote.
- 'Om built the right skills to take his career to Dubai's startup ecosystem.' The body adds 'at Mesa'. No source beyond the name and logo. The image does not say what the skills were.
- 'Architect → Founders Office'. A before and after career line. Checkable in principle, no source shown.
- '12 months', 'AI focused', 'Bangalore'. Programme facts.

## Offer and call to action
Apply to Mesa's PGP in Startup Leadership & Entrepreneurship. No price, scholarship or deadline is stated. Button on the image: APPLY NOW →. Ad button: Apply now.

## Why it may work
- A named person, cohort and employer make the outcome concrete and checkable.
- 'International' and 'Dubai' add a new benefit (a career abroad) that the other student statics do not offer.
- 'Architect → Founders Office' shows a switch from a non-business field in three words, which speaks to career changers.
- The candid mid-speech photo looks like real classroom activity, not a posed headshot.

## Where it is weak
- One student is an anecdote, not a rate. No number of international offers is given.
- No salary or seniority, and the employer is a small brand most viewers will not know.
- There is a lot of small text for a feed ad. The programme details at the bottom are easy to miss.
- He looks away from the camera, so the image has less stopping power than a direct-gaze portrait.
