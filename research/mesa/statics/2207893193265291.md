## Hook and message
Single idea: a hard revenue number on a short clock. For professionals who want to prove they can sell something, not only study. Promise: build and sell your first product and reach 5 lakh in sales within the first 8 weeks.

## Claims and proof
- 'Generate 5L in just 8 Weeks'. Worded as an outcome. Other Ascend ads describe ₹5L as a sales target, not a result. No source and no past cohort result shown.
- Body: 'Your first business could start in your first 8 weeks'. Softer wording than the image.
- Body: 'Early bird applications close Nov 26, 2026'. Deadline.
- Body: 'no application fee and scholarships of up to ₹10L'. Offer terms, no conditions shown.
- The classroom photo of a student pitching a product with a pricing slide is used as visual proof.

## Offer and call to action
Early bird applications close Nov 26, 2026, with no application fee and scholarships of up to ₹10L. All of that is in the body text only. Button on the image: Apply Now. Ad button: Apply now.

## Why it may work
- A number plus a time limit (5L, 8 weeks) is the most concrete promise in the Ascend set.
- The photo shows a real student product pitch with a real brand and prices, which makes the claim feel doable.
- The body stacks three reasons to act now: deadline, no fee, scholarship.

## Where it is weak
- '5L' with no rupee sign and no word 'revenue' or 'sales' can be misread as salary or profit.
- It is stated as a result when it is a target.
- The deadline and scholarship are missing from the image, where they would do the most work. The headline field ('Start your own business while you keep your job') does not match the image.
