How you learn: the case method
The Startup Case Methodology at Mesa
Read the case The founder’s problem and the data
Debate it in class Cold calls, trade-offs, no easy answers

#Case study · Sugarfit
Scaling personalisation without losing the human touch
Shivtosh Kumar Co-founder, Sugarfit
Sugarfit helps people control, and even reverse, diabetes through personalised programmes: continuous glucose monitoring, doctor-led plans and lifestyle coaching.
The question
Then Apple Fitness+ and Fitbit entered India. How does a local, human-centred startup stay ahead, and scale personalisation without losing empathy?
What students did
Analysed Sugarfit’s product data, customer metrics and competitive landscape
Debated the trade-offs between technology and the human touch
Built strategies to strengthen Sugarfit’s edge against global giants
#Case study · Neeman’s
Can a sustainable brand go mainstream?
Amar Preet Singh Co-founder & COO, Neeman’s
Neeman’s launched in 2018 with shoes made from merino wool, organic cotton and used tyres. By 2025 it had grown from online-only D2C to 20 exclusive stores, with sights set on 100.
The question
How does a sustainable D2C brand scale without losing its authenticity, and balance online control with offline reach?
What students did
Examined product, channel and customer data
Worked through purpose vs profit, and sustainability vs scale
Designed a five-year growth roadmap for the brand

Four builders, one flea-market stall
Ananta Khandelwal, Hasmitha Rangaswamy, Sushmitha Sarlana and Vasudeva Kashyap built XOCO from scratch in Cohort 4.

Saras Che · Crispy & bold
Two second-year undergraduates
Aaradhy Jani runs Mesa’s Leverage finance club. Arihant Jain, 18, had already spent nine years helping run his family business in Chhattisgarh.
The gap between what sells and what’s good
Most coin khakhra is palm oil and maida, sold loose and unlabelled. Saras Che uses rice bran oil and whole wheat, in 14 labelled flavours. It started on consignment in one store in JP Nagar, Bengaluru.
From one store to distribution
12+ stores reorder monthly, with zero ad spend. Samples at Mumbai’s CMPL Expo brought ₹90 lakh in leads and ₹40 lakh in orders, and a ₹60 lakh retail-chain contract is signed for FY27.
Where Saras Che stands now
A ₹2 crore-a-month pipeline of export and distributor leads, limited by supply.
“I want a company that runs on systems, not on me being present. If Saras Che still needs me in the room in five years, I have built a job, not a business.”
Aaradhy Jani, Co-founder, Saras Che

Frequently Asked Questions
Who is Ascend for? Working professionals with at least 5 years of full-time experience who want to keep their job while they grow. Most come with one of four goals: moving into leadership, changing direction, starting a business, or growing a family business.
How many hours a week will this take? About 10 hours. A live class on Saturday morning (2.5 hours), a founder session or lab on Sunday (1.5 hours), a team check-in on one weekday evening (1 hour), and about 5 hours of reading and project work whenever suits you. Weeks close to a sprint deadline can be busier.
What if I miss a class? Every class is recorded. The assignment for that class is still due, and your group will still expect your part.
How do you keep online classes rigorous? Cameras stay on, you read the case before class, and faculty cold-call. Every class has an assignment, and faculty and operators hold office hours when you are stuck.
How will I build a network if most of it is online? You start together on campus in Bangalore and come back through the year. In between there are city meetups with founders, operators and Mesa alumni, regular founder sessions where you work on a problem they are facing right now, and warm introductions based on what you need.
Do I need to live in Bangalore? No. Classes are online. You come to campus six times for 3 days each: orientation, four blocks for exams and assessments, and graduation. You may need to take leave for some of these. If you do live in Bangalore, you can also attend selected classes on campus.
Is this an MBA or a degree? Ascend is a postgraduate programme (PGP) from Mesa School of Business, like every Mesa programme. It is not a university degree.
Is there placement? There is no placement guarantee. Ascend is built for people who stay employed through the programme. What you do get is a career coach from month one, a curated job board from Mesa's hiring partners and alumni, 1:1 interview prep and mock interviews once you are shortlisted, and resume and LinkedIn reviews.
How is this different from an online AI or business course? A course gives you content. In Ascend you also run a real business with a ₹5L revenue target, ship an AI fix at your own job and work on live startup problems, with a cohort, mentors and time on campus.
I'm a developer. Will the AI part be too basic? There are beginner and advanced tracks, and the tool stack is refreshed every cohort. Everyone applies AI to their own problem, so the difficulty scales with what you choose to build.
I'm not technical. Will I keep up? Yes. The beginner track starts with AI for everyday productivity and no-code building. Your project is a problem you already understand from your own work.
What if I don't have a business idea? You don't need one. In the D2C sprint you choose from ideas we have already vetted. The Mesa Startup Lab is there if an idea of your own appears along the way.
Can I build my own startup during the programme? Yes. The Mesa Startup Lab takes you from idea to validation, MVP, launch and first customers with a 1:1 founder mentor, and ends with a pitch to VCs and angels at Demo Day. You test the idea while your salary keeps coming in.
I run a family business. Is there a track for me? Yes. A 1:1 mentor helps you diagnose the business and run one growth project, such as a new vertical, a D2C channel or digitisation. Your AI project can be built on a problem from your own business.
What does the fee cover? The fee is ₹22,00,000 to ₹23,00,000 depending on the round you apply in, plus 18% GST. It covers all classes, mentoring, ₹1L of starting capital for your BYOB team, career coaching and lifelong Mesa membership. Global immersions are paid separately, and you arrange your own travel and stay for campus visits.
Are there scholarships or loans? Yes. There are six scholarships for different profiles, including ex-founders, women leaders and veterans. Once you are offered a seat, Mesa's loan partners offer loans covering up to 100% of tuition, with tenures of up to 12 years.
Are the global immersions included in the fee? They are optional and paid separately, with pricing shared up front. Whether one destination is included is TBC.
What happens after the 12 months? Your Mesa membership continues for life: free bootcamps when AI and tools change, career support on every future move, access to mentors and the alumni network, and an open invitation back to campus classes.
