## Why it may work, and where it is weak
Works:
- The skit starts mid-argument with a couple, so there is movement and a human reaction in the first second; no logo or offer before it.
- A relatable domestic trigger (looking at the account balance) leads into a plain problem: idle savings treated as an "emergency fund".
- One product, one number (9-12%), three reassurances (sell anytime, Rs 1,000, 0 brokerage), one action.
- The legal strip and SEBI number are visible from frame one, which helps trust in a regulated category.
- Speaker looks at the lens and uses hand gestures, which keeps the long talking-head block lively.

Weak:
- First cut is at 10.5 s and the skit is not clearly tied to the product until about 22 s; viewers may not see the link.
- "Sell anytime, no lock-in" in the ad text is qualified on screen (buyer availability, before 2pm rule); the qualifying text is small and sits on screen for a short time.
- The headline figure is "up to 12%" with an asterisk and the video gives no example bond, issuer or rate to prove a number.
- The machine transcript suggests the voice-over wording around "on the table" and the 2 pm rule is easy to mishear; the spoken claim and the written disclaimer are not obviously aligned.
- About 13 s at the end is spent on a spoken legal block over a static end card; the call to action is spoken only once.
- The DCO setup means the same video may appear under different headlines, so results cannot be tied to this video alone.
